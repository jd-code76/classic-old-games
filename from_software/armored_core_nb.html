Brief History of Armored Core: Nine Breaker (2004)

Originally released in Japan on October 28th, 2004, Armored Core: Nine Breaker is the ninth entry in the Armored Core series and a spin-off to Armored Core: Nexus. It drops the story-based campaign entirely in favor of two main modes: a training program featuring over 150 exercises that test attack, defense, movement, and overall piloting skills, and an expansive arena mode with ranked opponents. Players build an AC from a parts pool of over 400 components and 150 airframes that combines elements from previous entries, then face off in versus-style combat. The game also features a web ranking mode and an auto-generation function for randomly built opponents. Its smarter AI, capable of dodging missiles, and the depth of its customization make it a favorite for tuning practice and honing fundamentals.

However, Nine Breaker is widely criticized for its lack of narrative content, a slow and confusing interface considered the worst in the series, long loading times, and the absence of online play (limited to system link only). The training missions suffer from steep and uneven difficulty spikes, and the repetitive loop of exercises and arena ladders can feel hollow without a campaign to provide context or a sense of accomplishment. It is generally not recommended as an entry point into the franchise. It should be noted that because players can import their save, starting a fresh Nine Breaker file and importing it into Last Raven provides a more usable parts selection than starting Last Raven from scratch, though importing a completed Nexus save remains the better option.
